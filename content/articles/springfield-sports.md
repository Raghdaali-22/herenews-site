**SPRINGFIELD, Va. —** The best high school volleyball player in Virginia this season played her home matches a few miles from the Beltway.

Anna Dierks, a 5-foot-10 senior setter at West Springfield High School, has been named the 2026 MaxPreps Virginia Player of the Year, the publication announced. The honor caps a season in which the Spartans went 33-0 and won the Class 6 state championship, the largest of Virginia's six classifications.

MaxPreps has named a top player in each state since 2008, weighing team success and individual performance along with local and state awards.

Dierks was the engine behind an offense that rarely stalled. In the state final, a sweep of Colonial Forge of Stafford, she handed out 34 assists while six different West Springfield hitters recorded a kill — the kind of distribution that defines a setter's value and doesn't always show up in a box score.

Her season totals were 914 assists, 190 digs, 126 aces, 70 kills and 26 blocks. Across three varsity seasons with the Spartans, she compiled 2,369 assists, 561 digs, 281 aces and 147 kills.

The numbers place her among the state's most productive setters, and the ace total — better than one per match — reflects a serve that pressured opponents rather than merely started rallies. Her 26 blocks, unusual volume for a setter, hint at a front-row presence that held up when she rotated to the net.

She is the first West Springfield player to take the award in at least the past five seasons, according to MaxPreps' list of recent winners, which includes Milan Rex of Alexandria City in 2022, Mickhaila Murray of Flint Hill in 2023, Caleigh Ponn of Hidden Valley in 2024 and Kennedy Spaid of James Wood in 2025.

The recognition puts Dierks in consideration for the MaxPreps All-America Team, which is scheduled for release in December. Selections for that team draw from the state players of the year.

For West Springfield, the award is the exclamation point on a perfect season — one that ended with a trophy and now with the state's top individual honor attached to the player who directed it.
