**SPRINGFIELD, Va. —** Springfield's Thanksgiving will be wet, windy and loud.

Heavy rain, the threat of a thunderstorm and wind strong enough to bring down trees are forecast across Fairfax County on Thursday, with AccuWeather warning of flooded streets and scattered power outages as the region settles in for the holiday. The high was expected to reach 55 degrees, with an overnight low of 48.

At 6:45 a.m., light rain was falling and the temperature stood at 46 degrees. The RealFeel, which factors in wind and dampness, was 38, with a northeast wind at 9 mph gusting to 23 mph. AccuWeather's MinuteCast called for rain to continue for at least another hour, and its hourly forecast put the chance of precipitation near 90% from midmorning through the afternoon.

A wind advisory for Fairfax County was to remain in effect until 7 p.m., and AccuWeather listed coastal flood advisories for the area. Forecasters said the combination of saturated ground and gusty wind could topple trees and take down power lines, and that low-lying roads may pond quickly.

Daylight is short: the sun rose at 7:03 a.m. and sets at 4:49 p.m., a span of 9 hours, 46 minutes.

### The mall is closed; Friday comes early

Springfield Town Center, the county's largest shopping destination and the anchor of the Springfield Mall area, is closed for the holiday, according to its hours page. It reopens at 9 a.m. Friday — three hours ahead of its normal 10 a.m. start — for the traditional post-Thanksgiving rush.

Regular hours resume Saturday, 10 a.m. to 9 p.m., and Sunday, 11 a.m. to 7 p.m. Individual store and restaurant hours vary, and shoppers heading out Friday should confirm with a specific retailer before making the trip.

For those who take the train, the Franconia-Springfield Metro station on the Blue Line is the nearest rail link; walkers can reach the mall entrance by Dick's Sporting Goods in about 15 minutes. Drivers have two on-site garages — one near Macy's, reached from Frontier Road, and one near Firestone Complete Auto Care, reached from Loisdale Road — plus EV charging at several locations around the property. Valet service near Yard House operates Friday through Sunday.

### Advice for residents

* **Stay off the roads during the heaviest rain.**

   If you must drive, slow down, use headlights and turn around rather than through standing water. Even shallow, fast-moving water can float a car.
* **Charge phones and power banks now.**

   Dig out flashlights and a battery-powered radio, and keep fresh batteries on hand. Candles are a poor substitute on a windy night.
* **Tie down what can blow away.**

   Trash cans, recycling bins, inflatable decorations and light lawn furniture should be brought inside or weighted down before gusts pick up.
* **Treat downed lines as live.**

   Stay back, keep children and pets away and call 911. Report outages to your electric utility.
* **Protect the food.**

   If the power fails, keep refrigerator and freezer doors shut. A full freezer holds its temperature for roughly two days.
* **Never run a generator indoors**

   or in a garage, and keep portable generators well away from windows and doors. Carbon monoxide kills quietly.
* **Check on neighbors**

   who live alone, are older or rely on electric medical equipment, and bring pets inside.
* **Take the turkey fryer seriously.**

   Never fry indoors or in a garage, and keep the rig out of the rain. A wet or frozen bird dropped into hot oil can set off a violent boil-over.

### Advice for visitors

Families driving in from out of town should pad their travel time, watch for ponding on low-lying stretches and check the state's 511 service before leaving. Travelers flying out of Reagan National or Dulles should confirm flight status with their airline, since strong wind can ripple through schedules.

And anyone planning to shop Thursday will find the doors locked at Springfield Town Center. Those arriving by Metro should check the holiday schedule before heading to the Franconia-Springfield station.

The holiday's marquee events in Springfield are mostly indoors and mostly Friday: ESIO Board Game Night at 6:30 p.m. at 6509 Springfield Mall, IGNITE '26 at 7 p.m. at Hotel Belvoir on Loisdale Road and music bingo at 7 p.m. at Dave & Buster's. "Together in Light, Songs of the Season" follows Dec. 6 at Hayfield Secondary School.

### Relief is on the way

Friday turns mostly cloudy and breezy with a high of 51 and a low of 42, and only a 25% chance of rain, according to AccuWeather. Saturday looks like the weekend's best window: partly sunny, not as cool, 59 degrees and a low of 34 — good weather for yard cleanup, outdoor decorating or a walk. Sunday is pleasant at 60 with evening showers possible.

### Also in Springfield

**Toy drive underway.** Sheehy Auto Stores is collecting new, unwrapped toys through Dec. 11 at three Springfield-area dealerships — the Ford and Subaru stores on Loisdale Road and the Volkswagen store on Backlick Road — during regular business hours. The toys go to local children, including teenagers, through community partners.

**A state honor for West Springfield.** Spartans senior Anna Dierks was named the 2026 MaxPreps Virginia High School Volleyball Player of the Year after leading West Springfield to a 33-0 season and the Class 6 state championship. The setter had 34 assists in the title match and is eligible for December's All-America selections.

**Second homes get easier.** Fairfax County supervisors loosened rules for accessory living units, dropping owner-occupancy and extra-parking requirements and simplifying some approvals while keeping building standards intact. Supervisor Pat Herrity backed the change but raised concerns about cumulative effects on neighborhoods.
