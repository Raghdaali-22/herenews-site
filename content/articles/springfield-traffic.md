SPRINGFIELD, Va. — Motorists in Springfield should plan around one of the nation's worst highway bottlenecks this week, according to guidance from the I-95 Exit Guide, which warns drivers to avoid rush hours on the capital beltway corridor "at all costs."

The guide identifies the weekday rush windows as 7 to 9:30 a.m. and 3:30 to 7 p.m., when the stretch of Interstate 95 through Springfield is "notoriously congested." Just north of town sits the interchange of I-95, I-395 and I-495 — known locally as the "mixing bowl" — which the guide ranks among the worst bottlenecks in the United States.

Drivers in a hurry have options, the guide notes. The Express Lanes that run alongside I-95 and I-395 accept E-ZPass only. Alternative routes include U.S. Route 1, which runs parallel to I-95 and can be faster during peak times, and the Capital Beltway for travelers bypassing Washington entirely.

For stops along the way, the guide points to Springfield Town Center at Exit 169A for food, gas and restrooms, and the Franconia-Springfield Metro station at Exit 169 as a place to "ditch your car" and take the train into the District.

Enforcement is a hazard of its own, the guide cautions. Virginia State Police maintain an aggressive speed enforcement posture, and drivers are advised not to exceed posted limits by more than 9 mph. Driving 80 mph or 20 mph over the limit qualifies as reckless driving — a criminal offense in Virginia, not merely a traffic ticket.

Commuters should also find relief from construction: the I-95 Exit Guide's newsroom reported that the Virginia Department of Transportation plans to lift most lane closures for Thanksgiving travel. Recent closures near Fredericksburg, about 40 miles south, had been scheduled through late November.

**Weather glimpse:** Skies were partly cloudy early Monday with a temperature in the low 50s, according to The Weather Channel's hourly forecast. The day was forecast to turn mostly cloudy with an afternoon high near 68 degrees and winds out of the northwest at 8 to 9 mph. Rain chances were low, peaking at 15% — a drier ride than Sunday night's showers left behind.
