SPRINGFIELD, Va. (Nov. 8) Springfield is represented in the U.S. Senate by two Democrats, Mark Warner and Tim Kaine, and only one of those seats was on the ballot in this month's midterm elections.



Warner, seeking a fourth term, defeated Republican Bert Mizusawa, a retired Army major general, by roughly 20 percentage points on Nov. 3, according to unofficial results. Warner took about 60% of the vote to Mizusawa's 39%.



The 71-year-old senator has said this term will be his last. "This is my last campaign," Warner told supporters at an election night event in Alexandria, adding that he would spend the coming years pressing for a single-payer health insurance system, more affordable homeownership and new guardrails on artificial intelligence.



Kaine, Virginia's junior senator, was not on the ballot. He won a third term in 2024 and is not up for reelection until 2030.



Springfield's U.S. House representation was also on the ballot. Most of the community sits in Virginia's 11th Congressional District, where Rep. James Walkinshaw won reelection, while a portion lies in the 8th District, held by Rep. Don Beyer, who won a seventh term.



Beyond the federal races, Springfield voters joined the rest of Virginia in approving three constitutional amendments, according to unofficial results. They add a right to reproductive health care, including abortion, to the state constitution; remove now-unenforceable language barring same-sex marriage; and restore voting rights to people who have completed sentences for felonies. The amendments take effect Jan. 1.



Fairfax County voters also approved two bond questions: $180 million for parks and park facilities and $75 million for community health and human services. Each passed with about 70% support.



No Springfield-specific state or county offices were on the 2026 ballot. Virginia holds its General Assembly and local elections in odd-numbered years, and those seats are next up in 2027.



Until then, Springfield is represented in Richmond by Sen. Dave Marsden, a Democrat whose 35th Senate District includes the community, and Del. Kathy Tran, a Democrat from the 18th House District. On the Fairfax County Board of Supervisors, Springfield District Supervisor Pat Herrity, a Republican, holds the seat and has said he will seek a sixth term next year. Sandy Anderson represents the Springfield District on the county School Board. Gov. Abigail Spanberger, a Democrat elected in 2025, took office in January.
